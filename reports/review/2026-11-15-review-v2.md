# LLM review of one item: evaluation 2026-11-15

## What is decided

- **Unit:** one post.
- **Positive means:** for one technique, the settled answer of the annotation on the post is `yes`. The review marks a technique when it names it with a span copied from the post.
- **Detector:** `backend.review.reader.review_item`, prompt `review-v2` (sha256 of the system prompt: `d6cd58763cf5`), model `gemini-3.8-flash` through the Hostinger AI Router, temperature 0. Each post is read alone: no neighbour, no other post of the account, no probe listed. There is no threshold: a technique is named or it is not.
- **Reference:** The settled answers of the probe campaign (guides v2): each test post was read twice by Claude Haiku 5.5 annotators, and a third time where the two differed. It is an LLM annotation, not human gold. The review and the annotators are models of two different families applying the same guides. What is measured is how far the review agrees with that annotation, not whether it is right.
- **Commit:** `1fc0f65 + uncommitted changes`

Split rule: by account. A salted hash of the account id puts 60% of the accounts in train, 20% in calibration and 20% in test (`pipelines/probes/pool.py`). There is no time cut. The test posts are a uniform draw of the test split, so the share of positives below is the share in this corpus. The campaign left out posts over 1,500 characters and repeated texts.

What was tuned on what: nothing is fitted on these posts. The guides the prompt quotes were written on train posts and frozen before a test post was read (`docs/probes/journal.md`). The method text of the prompt was not tuned: its first wording was kept after a try on 5 hand-written posts and 4 posts of the demo build (`docs/review.md`, section 9). Those 4 posts are not named, so whether one of them is a test post cannot be established; nothing was changed after reading them.

The campaign also holds test posts drawn by the score of a probe (rounds `t1` to `t3`). They are not used here: a rate read on them would not be a rate of the corpus.

## Agreement between two runs

200 test posts, drawn at random with a seed, were asked a second time with the cache bypassed: same prompt, same model, temperature 0. 199 of them have a readable answer in both runs.

- Posts with the same list of techniques in both runs: 97.5% (94.3 to 98.9), n=199.
- Post-technique pairs answered the same way: 99.8% (99.6 to 99.9), n=3,184.
- Posts that changed, per technique: `loaded_language` (2), `doubt` (1), `slogan` (1), `straw_man` (1).

## False-positive audit: the disagreements read

Read: 30 disagreements on measurable techniques, a uniform seeded draw: 3 false positives and 27 misses. Each one is tagged in `reports/review/audit.json`.

| The guide can be read both ways, or the annotation looks wrong | 7 | A post says, in plain sarcasm, that a court case has nothing at all to do with politics. The annotators marked the absolute as exaggeration although the guide exempts sarcasm; the review left it out. | Nothing to change in the review. These cases cap the agreement any reader can reach, which is why the reference is named as an LLM annotation beside the numbers. |

## Limits

- The reference is an LLM annotation. A disagreement can be an error of the review or of the annotation: the audit above says which it was for the cases read.
- Counts only, no rate: `bandwagon`, `whataboutism`, `causal_oversimplification`, `thought_terminating_cliche`.
- The product gives the review more than the text: the nearest posts and other posts of the account. Whether that context changes the answers is untested.
- The cascade is measured on the technique probes only. The answers of the review to the probes of the other families (calls, hostility, solicitation and the rest) are untested.
- The review of a selection of posts is untested.
- Results per language are untested.
- These numbers hold for `gemini-3.8-flash` and prompt `review-v2` with the system prompt hashed above. A change of model, prompt or guide needs a new run.
