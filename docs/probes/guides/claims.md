# Guide: claims

Version: v2. Read `common.md` first.

This guide has four labels. A post can get several `yes`, or none.

Rule 3 of `common.md` (own voice) does not apply to `checkable_claim` and `hearsay`: a reported statement can hold a claim.

Evidence for every label of this guide: required.

## `checkable_claim`

**Question.** Does the post state a fact about the world that could be checked?

**YES when all are true:**

1. The post contains a declarative statement of what is or was the case. Not an opinion, a taste, a forecast, a question, a wish or an order.
2. The statement is specific: it names who or what, and a concrete fact about it: an event, an act, a number, a date, a place, a record, a cause, or words attributed to someone.
3. The statement is about the world outside the author's private life: people in public roles, organisations, places, events, society, the economy, science, health, products.

**NO when:**

- the statement is a value judgement or a generality ("politicians always lie", "this law is unfair");
- the statement is about the future ("prices will double");
- the statement is about the author's own life ("I had pizza tonight").

Whether the statement is true does not matter. A news headline that states an event is a checkable claim.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Unemployment rose to 5.2% in May" → `Unemployment rose to 5.2% in May`
- YES: "The mayor voted against the housing plan last week" → `The mayor voted against the housing plan last week`
- YES: "This vaccine causes heart damage in children" → `This vaccine causes heart damage in children`
- NO: "This government is the worst we ever had" (a value judgement)
- NO: "They will raise taxes after the election, you'll see" (the future)

## `unsourced_statistic`

**Question.** Does the post give a statistic with no source?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post asserts, as a real measurement or count about a population, society, the economy, health, science, an organisation or an event, a number of the kind: a percentage, a rate, a count of people or cases, an amount of money, a multiple ("doubled", "3 out of 4").
2. The post names no source for it: no organisation, study, publication or official body, no "according to …", and no URL.

**NO when:**

- a source is named, or the post contains a URL;
- the number is a price in an offer, a sports score, a date, a time, an age, or a quantity from the author's own life;
- the number is a threshold, a target, a hypothesis or a figure of speech ("they need 51% to pass it", "a million times better").

**Examples.**

- YES: "70% of teachers want to quit. Let that sink in" → `70% of teachers want to quit`
- YES: "Two million people were at the march yesterday" → `Two million people were at the march yesterday`
- NO: "70% of teachers want to quit, according to a union survey" (a source is named)
- NO: "Only $9.99 this weekend" (a price in an offer)

## `hearsay`

**Question.** Does the post pass on information marked as heard from unnamed others?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post passes on a piece of information.
2. It marks it explicitly as rumour or as heard from unnamed others: "I heard that", "I'm hearing", "people are saying", "they say", "word is", "rumour has it", "apparently", "supposedly", "sources say", "a friend told me", "someone told me".

**NO when:**

- the source is named ("the BBC reports");
- the only marker is "allegedly" or "reportedly" in a news-style sentence.

**Examples.**

- YES: "I'm hearing the factory will close before Christmas" → `I'm hearing the factory will close`
- YES: "Apparently the minister is about to resign" → `Apparently the minister is about to resign`
- NO: "The BBC reports the factory will close" (a named source)

## `urgency_framing`

**Question.** Does the post frame its content as urgent, scarce or about to disappear?

**YES when:**

1. The post contains an explicit marker of at least one kind:
   - urgency: "BREAKING", "URGENT", "ALERT", "JUST IN", "act now", "hurry", "right now" as an order, a 🚨 opening the post;
   - a deadline or scarcity: "last chance", "ends tonight", "only 3 left", "limited time", "before it's too late";
   - suppression: "before it gets deleted", "they don't want you to see this", "share before it's taken down".

**NO when:**

- a time is only mentioned ("the meeting is on Friday");
- the post reports an urgent event with no marker ("Fire crews are at the scene").

**Examples.**

- YES: "🚨 BREAKING: the minister has resigned" → `🚨 BREAKING`
- YES: "Watch this before they take it down" → `before they take it down`
- YES: "Last chance: sale ends tonight" → `Last chance`
- NO: "The sale runs from Monday to Friday" (a time is mentioned)
