# Guide: subject

Version: v2. Read `common.md` first.

This guide has seven labels. Five take `yes`, `no` or `unclear`. `theme` takes one theme. `political_side` takes `left`, `right` or `none`.

Evidence: required for `anti_pluralism`, `institution_distrust`, `conspiracy_narrative` and `endorsement`. None for `political`, `theme` and `political_side`.

## `political`

**Question.** Is the post about political life or a public policy?

**YES when:**

1. The post is about at least one of: a government, a party, an election, a politician in their role, a law, a bill or a court ruling on a public issue, a public policy debate (migration, taxes, healthcare, climate, policing, education, foreign affairs), a war or diplomacy, a protest or activism, a political ideology.

**NO when:**

- the post is only personal, commercial, sport, entertainment or weather;
- the post is crime, business, science or health news with no government, law or policy in it ("Man arrested for burglary in Leeds", "Acme profits up 10%");
- the post complains about life with no government, law or policy in it ("everything is so expensive now");
- the post names a person and nothing in it shows a political role. Answer `no`, not `unclear`.

**Examples.**

- YES: "The new asylum law passed by 12 votes"
- YES: "Prices keep rising and this government does nothing"
- NO: "Prices keep rising, I can't afford eggs anymore"
- NO: "What a goal by the captain tonight"

## `theme`

**Question.** What is the main theme of the post?

**Answer with one theme.** Read the list from the top and answer the **first** theme that fits the main statement of the post.

| Theme | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| `war_geopolitics` | Armed conflicts, armies, terrorism, diplomacy, relations between states, sanctions |
| `migration` | Immigration, asylum, refugees, borders, deportation, integration |
| `crime_justice` | Crime, police, courts, trials, prisons, public safety |
| `health` | Diseases, medicine, vaccines, healthcare, mental health, drugs, nutrition |
| `climate_energy` | Climate, environment, pollution, weather, natural disasters, energy |
| `economy_work` | Economy, prices, taxes, jobs, wages, housing, companies, markets, finance, crypto, things for sale |
| `society_identity` | Gender, sexuality, feminism, abortion, race and racism, family, education, generations |
| `religion` | Faith, churches, scripture, religious practice, religious holidays |
| `media_tech` | Journalism, media outlets, social platforms, internet, AI, software, gadgets, science, space |
| `politics_government` | Parties, elections, politicians, governments, parliaments, ideologies, when no theme above fits |
| `sport` | Sports, athletes, matches, sports betting |
| `entertainment_culture` | Music, film, TV, celebrities, games, books, art, fashion, fandoms |
| `everyday_personal` | Personal life, feelings, relationships, greetings, daily routine, food, travel, pets |
| `other` | None of the above, or impossible to tell |

**Examples.**

- "The new asylum law passed by 12 votes" → `migration` (it fits before `politics_government`)
- "Lopez leads the polls ahead of Sunday's vote" → `politics_government`
- "Earn $500 a day trading crypto" → `economy_work`
- "Good morning everyone, coffee time ☕" → `everyday_personal`

## `political_side`

**Question.** Does the post take the side of the political left or the political right?

**Answer `none` unless the post takes a side shown by at least one marker below.**

| Answer | Markers |
| --- | --- |
| `right` | Attacks or mocks "the left", "liberals", "leftists", "socialists", "communists", "woke", "antifa", or a left-wing party or politician. Or supports "conservatives", "patriots", or a right-wing party or politician. Or argues for: less immigration, deportations, national sovereignty, traditional family or religious values in law, gun rights, lower taxes, a smaller state; or against "gender ideology" or "climate hysteria" |
| `left` | Attacks or mocks "the right", "conservatives", "fascists", "the far right", "capitalists", "billionaires", or a right-wing party or politician. Or supports "progressives", "socialists", or a left-wing party or politician. Or argues for: taxing the rich, redistribution, workers' rights, unions, welcoming migrants, anti-racism, feminism, LGBT rights, abortion rights, climate action, police reform |
| `none` | No marker. Or markers of both sides. Or you do not know where the party or the politician stands |

**Rules.**

1. A position on a foreign conflict is not a marker.
2. Criticising a government is not a marker by itself.
3. Reporting what a side says is not taking that side (rule 3 of `common.md`).

**Examples.**

- "The woke mob wants to cancel Christmas now" → `right`
- "Tax the billionaires, fund the hospitals" → `left`
- "This government is useless" → `none`
- "The conservative party published its manifesto today" → `none`

## `anti_pluralism`

**Question.** Does the post want opponents to be denied the right to exist, speak, vote or govern?

**YES when:**

1. The post demands or approves at least one of: banning a party, a movement or a media outlet; jailing, deporting or eliminating people for their opinions or their camp; taking away the right to vote or to speak from a group; ending elections; rule by one party or one leader with no opposition.

**NO when:**

- the post demands prosecution for a specific alleged crime ("he should be tried for fraud");
- the post wants someone to resign or to lose an election;
- the post only criticises or insults opponents.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Ban this party and jail its leaders. They have no place here" → `Ban this party and jail its leaders`
- YES: "People who vote for them shouldn't be allowed to vote at all" → `shouldn't be allowed to vote at all`
- NO: "The minister should be tried for the bribes he took" (a specific alleged crime)
- NO: "I can't wait to see this party lose in June" (an election)

## `institution_distrust`

**Question.** Does the post state that a whole institution lies, is corrupt or is rigged?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The target is an institution or a system as a whole: elections or the electoral system, the courts or the justice system, "the media" or "the press", science, medicine or academia, health agencies, official statistics, the intelligence services, "the system", "the deep state".
2. The post asserts that it lies, is corrupt, rigged, bought, captured or fake.

**NO when:**

- the target is one named outlet, one judge, one study or one decision ("this article is wrong", "judge Hall is biased");
- the target is a government, a party or a politician;
- the post reports that others distrust the institution.

**Examples.**

- YES: "The media lie to you every single day" → `The media lie to you every single day`
- YES: "Elections here are rigged, voting changes nothing" → `Elections here are rigged`
- NO: "The Daily Post got this story wrong" (one outlet)
- NO: "This government lies about everything" (a government)

## `conspiracy_narrative`

**Question.** Does the post explain something by the secret plan of a powerful group?

**YES when all are true:**

1. The post explains an event or a situation by a secret plan, a staging or a coordinated cover-up.
2. It attributes this to a powerful group or network: "they", the elites, a government, globalists, an industry, a cabal, the intelligence services, an ethnic or religious group.
3. The author asserts it or strongly insinuates it. Markers: "planned", "staged", "false flag", "psyop", "cover-up", "they don't want you to know", "it's all scripted".

**NO when:**

- the post accuses named individuals of an ordinary crime or of corruption ("the mayor took bribes");
- the post criticises lobbying or influence that is done in the open;
- the post reports, debunks or mocks such a theory.

**Examples.**

- YES: "The outage was no accident. They planned it to test how we'd react" → `They planned it to test how we'd react`
- YES: "Big Pharma is hiding the cure because sick people make them rich" → `Big Pharma is hiding the cure`
- NO: "The mayor is accused of taking bribes from a builder" (an ordinary crime)
- NO: "No, the moon landing was not staged. Here is why" (a debunk)

## `endorsement`

**Question.** Does the post support or praise a named public person, organisation, brand or product?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post names a person, a party, an organisation, a brand or a product: by name, by `@name`, or by a unique title ("the president"). A named person counts unless the post shows a private tie ("my sister", "my friend Tom").
2. The author expresses support, praise, admiration or thanks for it: "the best mayor we ever had", "proud to support X", "X is doing an amazing job", "love this brand", "thank you @X for standing up".

**NO when:**

- the one praised is shown as a private tie (a friend, a relative), or is not named;
- the thanks are automatic or social ("thanks for the follow");
- the post reports that others support it ("thousands cheered the president").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Lopez is the only candidate who actually listens. She has my full support" → `She has my full support`
- YES: "Honestly the Acme X2 is the best phone ever made" → `the best phone ever made`
- NO: "My sister is the kindest person I know" (a private person)
- NO: "Crowds cheered Lopez in the capital today" (a report)
