# Guide: hostility

Version: v2. Read `common.md` first.

This guide has nine labels. A post can get several `yes`, or none.

## Shared definition D: demeaning content

Four labels of this guide (`attack_addressee`, `attack_person`, `attack_identity_group`, `attack_political_group`) need demeaning content D aimed at a target. D is at least one of:

| Form | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| An insult or a slur | "idiot", "scum", "clown", "moron" |
| A word that brands the target itself | "liar", "crook", "fraud", "traitor", "coward", "racist", "fascist" |
| A statement that the target is worthless, stupid, evil or disgusting | "these people are garbage", "she is a disgrace" |
| A negative judgement of the target's character, said of who they are | "arrogant", "incompetent", "dishonest", "pathetic", "misogynist" |
| Mockery of a personal trait | looks, voice, name, weight, disability |
| Illness or senility used as a put-down | "demented", "senile", "psycho", "his dementia" |

The test: is the negative word said of **who the target is** (D), or of something the target **did, said, decided or made** (not D)?

D is **not**:

- criticism of an act, a decision, a statement, an idea, a policy or a piece of work, however harsh: "this law is a disaster", "his speech was full of lies", "terrible decision";
- an accusation of a specific act, made with a verb: "he lied about the budget", "she took bribes". Compare: "he is a liar" brands the person and is D;
- an insult aimed at the author themselves ("I'm such an idiot");
- an insult aimed at a thing, a film, a product, a place or a sports team ("this movie is trash").

Evidence for every label of this guide: required.

## `attack_addressee`

**Question.** Does the post demean the person or people it speaks to?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post speaks to someone in the second person ("you", "your", "u", an imperative), or opens with an `@name` and has no third-person verb about that account.
2. It contains D aimed at that someone, or a contemptuous dismissal: "shut up", "stfu", "get lost", "nobody asked you", "cry more".

**NO when:**

- "you" is generic ("you can't trust anyone these days");
- the insult is obviously playful between friends (rule 4 of `common.md`).

**Examples.**

- YES: "You're a clown and everybody knows it" → `You're a clown`
- YES: "@dave shut up, nobody asked you" → `shut up, nobody asked you`
- NO: "You are wrong about the numbers, check the report" (criticism of a statement)
- NO: "@dave is a clown" (third person: that is `attack_person`)

## `attack_person`

**Question.** Does the post demean an identifiable individual it speaks about?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post speaks about one individual in the third person, identified by a name, an `@name`, or a unique role ("the president", "our mayor", "the CEO of Acme").
2. It contains D aimed at that individual.

**NO when:**

- the individual is not identifiable ("my neighbour", "this guy", "such a person", "some idiot at the store"). Answer `no`, not `unclear`;
- the target is a group (see the two group labels).

**Examples.**

- YES: "The mayor is a corrupt little coward" → `corrupt little coward`
- YES: "@dave is a clown" → `is a clown`
- NO: "The mayor's plan will bankrupt the city" (criticism of a policy)
- NO: "Some moron cut me off on the highway" (not identifiable)

## `attack_identity_group`

**Question.** Does the post demean a group defined by who its members are?

**YES when either is true:**

1. The post contains D aimed at a group, or at its members as such, defined by race or ethnicity, nationality or national origin, religion, gender, sexual orientation or gender identity, disability, or migrant or refugee status.
2. The post uses a slur for one of those groups, whoever it is aimed at.

**NO when:**

- the group is defined by politics, profession or age ("liberals", "journalists", "boomers");
- the post criticises a policy, a government or a religion's doctrine without demeaning its people.

**Examples.**

- YES: "These migrants are filthy criminals, all of them" → `filthy criminals, all of them`
- YES: "Women are too stupid to drive" → `too stupid to drive`
- NO: "The new immigration law is far too lax" (criticism of a policy)
- NO: "Boomers are clueless about tech" (age)

## `attack_political_group`

**Question.** Does the post demean a group defined by its politics, its profession or its institution?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The target is a collective defined by politics, profession or membership: a party, its voters or supporters, "the left", "the right", "liberals", "conservatives", activists, journalists, "the media", politicians, the police, judges, scientists, "the elites", a government, an organisation, a company.
2. The post contains D aimed at that collective or at its members as such.

**NO when:**

- the target is one individual (that is `attack_person`);
- the post criticises what the collective did or decided, without D.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Journalists are spineless liars" → `spineless liars`
- YES: "The left is a bunch of brainwashed sheep" → `a bunch of brainwashed sheep`
- NO: "The party voted against the bill again" (a report)
- NO: "Acme raised prices twice this year, unacceptable" (criticism of an act)

## `dehumanisation`

**Question.** Does the post describe people as less than human?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post speaks about a person or a group of people.
2. It says what kind of being they are, and that kind is not human: animals, vermin or insects ("these animals", "rats", "cockroaches", "an infestation", "they breed"); a disease ("a cancer", "a plague", "parasites"); filth or waste ("human garbage", "scum of the earth"); non-humans ("subhuman", "not even human", "things").

**NO when:**

- the comparison is flattering or neutral ("a lion on the pitch", "busy as a bee");
- the wording is a set phrase about one trait or one behaviour, not about what the target is: "bird-brained", "lapdog", "stubborn as a mule", "dumb as a rock", "chicken", "snake";
- the target is not people.

**Examples.**

- YES: "They are cockroaches and should be treated as such" → `They are cockroaches`
- YES: "This party is a cancer on our country" → `a cancer on our country`
- NO: "He played like a beast tonight" (flattering)
- NO: "The minister is the president's lapdog" (a set phrase about one behaviour)
- NO: "The stadium was a pigsty after the match" (a place)

## `threat`

**Question.** Does the author announce that they, or their group, will harm someone?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The subject is the author or the author's group ("I", "we"), stated or plainly implied.
2. What is announced is physical harm, death, damage to property, or intimidation of the kind "I know where you live". A condition does not cancel it ("if you come here, I'll break your legs").

**NO when:**

- the announced act is legal or digital: sue, report, block, expose a lie;
- the harm has no author: "you'll get what's coming", "karma will find you", "you'll regret this";
- it is obviously playful (rule 4 of `common.md`).

**Examples.**

- YES: "Keep talking and I'll break your jaw" → `I'll break your jaw`
- YES: "We know where you live. Sleep with one eye open" → `We know where you live`
- NO: "I'll sue you for this" (a legal act)
- NO: "One day you'll pay for what you did" (no author of the harm)

## `violence_endorsement`

**Question.** Does the post wish, celebrate or justify violence or a death, without asking anyone to commit it?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post is about physical violence, a death or a serious injury suffered by a person or a group, done or to be done by others or by events.
2. The author wishes it ("I hope he dies"), celebrates it ("glad he got shot", "good riddance"), justifies it ("they had it coming"), or praises those who did it.

**NO when:**

- the post asks someone to commit the violence, or the author announces their own violence;
- the post supports a legal penalty (prison, a fine);
- the harm wished is not physical ("hope he loses his job");
- the post reports violence without approving it.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Hope that guy gets hit by a bus" → `Hope that guy gets hit by a bus`
- YES: "He got beaten up? Good. He had it coming" → `Good. He had it coming`
- NO: "Three people were injured in the attack" (a report)
- NO: "I hope she gets fired for this" (not physical)

## `doxxing`

**Question.** Does the post publish private information that lets readers find or reach an individual?

**YES when both are true:**

1. The post is about an individual other than the author.
2. It gives that individual's home address, phone number, personal email, licence plate, identity number, or the place where a private person lives or works.

**NO when:**

- the contact belongs to a business, a public office or a public service;
- the author gives their own contact.

**Examples.**

- YES: "This is the guy from the video. He lives at 14 Oak Street, Springfield" → `He lives at 14 Oak Street, Springfield`
- NO: "Call the town hall on 555-0100 to complain" (a public office)
- NO: "DM me or text 555-0199 for prices" (the author's own contact)

## `profanity`

**Question.** Does the post contain a vulgar word?

**YES when:**

1. The post contains at least one vulgar or obscene word, in any language, written in full, censored ("f*ck", "sh!t") or abbreviated ("wtf", "tf", "stfu", "af", "asf", "lmfao", "bs", "fk", "mf"). A slur counts.

Quote the vulgar word exactly as the post writes it.

**NO when:**

- the only strong words are mild: "damn", "hell", "crap", "heck".

**Examples.**

- YES: "What the fuck is this" → `fuck`
- YES: "wtf is going on" → `wtf`
- NO: "Damn, that was close" (mild)
