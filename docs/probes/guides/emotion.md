# Guide: emotion

Version: v2. Read `common.md` first.

This guide has four labels. Each asks whether **the author expresses** an emotion in the wording of the post. A post can get several `yes`, or none.

## Shared condition E: the emotion is expressed in the wording

Every label of this guide needs condition E. E is met when the wording of the post shows the author's own emotion: an emotion word, an exclamation, an emoji, an insult, or a tone that leaves no doubt.

E is **not** met when:

- the post states a sad, alarming or happy fact in a neutral tone ("Three people died in the fire");
- the emotion belongs to someone else, quoted or reported ("Residents are furious");
- the emotion is only what a reader might feel.

A neutral or purely informative post gets `no` on all four labels.

Evidence for every label of this guide: none.

## `anger`

**Question.** Does the author express anger or indignation?

**YES when both are true:**

1. E is met.
2. The emotion shown is anger, outrage, indignation or exasperation: "furious", "outraged", "sick of", "fed up", "how dare they", "this is disgusting", angry swearing, an accusation shouted with exclamation marks, 😡 🤬.

**NO when:**

- the author disagrees calmly ("I think this is a mistake").

**Examples.**

- YES: "I am SO sick of these people lying to our faces!!"
- YES: "How dare they close the only hospital in town 😡"
- NO: "I don't agree with closing the hospital"

## `fear`

**Question.** Does the author express fear, worry or alarm?

**YES when both are true:**

1. E is met.
2. The emotion shown is fear, worry, anxiety or alarm: "I'm scared", "terrifying", "really worried", "this is alarming", "be very afraid", 😨 😱.

**NO when:**

- the post gives a calm warning or advice ("roads are icy, drive carefully").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Honestly terrified of what happens to us if this passes"
- YES: "This should scare every parent 😱"
- NO: "Storm expected tonight, close your shutters"

## `contempt`

**Question.** Does the author express contempt or mockery?

**YES when both are true:**

1. E is met.
2. The emotion shown is contempt, scorn or mockery toward someone or something: sneering, ridicule, belittling, "pathetic", "what a joke", "lol imagine thinking that", 🤡 🙄.

**NO when:**

- the humour has no target ("my cat just fell off the sofa 😂");
- the author criticises without looking down ("this plan has three flaws").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Imagine still believing this clown 🤡"
- YES: "What a joke of a government, truly pathetic"
- NO: "The plan has flaws but it is a start"

## `enthusiasm`

**Question.** Does the author express enthusiasm, joy or admiration?

**YES when both are true:**

1. E is met.
2. The emotion shown is enthusiasm, joy, excitement, admiration, pride or gratitude: "so excited", "amazing!", "can't wait", "love this", "congrats", "so proud", 🎉 😍 🔥.

**NO when:**

- the post is positive but flat ("the event went well");
- the thanks are automatic ("thanks for the follow").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Can't wait for Saturday, this is going to be amazing!! 🎉"
- YES: "So proud of this team, what a season 😍"
- NO: "The concert starts at 8pm"
