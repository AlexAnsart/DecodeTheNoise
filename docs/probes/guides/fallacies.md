# Guide: fallacies

Version: v2. Read `common.md` first.

This guide has nine labels. A post can get several `yes`, or none.

## Shared condition S: the post is about a public matter

Every label of this guide needs condition S. S is met when the post is about politics, government, a public policy, society, the economy, health, science, security, war, the media, an institution, or the conduct of a company or a public figure.

S is **not** met by a post that is only about the author's private life, entertainment, sport, hobbies, a product for sale, or a greeting.

If S is not met, answer `no` to all nine labels.

Evidence for every label of this guide: required.

## `bandwagon`

**Question.** Does the post use "many people think or do it" as the reason it is right?

**YES when all are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post says that many, most or all people believe, do or support something: "everyone knows", "millions agree", "the whole country is saying", "nobody believes that anymore".
3. It uses that as the reason the thing is true or should be done.

**NO when:**

- the post reports a poll or a figure and draws no conclusion ("62% support the bill, poll finds");
- "everyone" is not a reason for anything ("everyone is talking about the debate").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Everyone knows the election was rigged, stop pretending" → `Everyone knows the election was rigged`
- YES: "Millions have already switched sides. They can't all be wrong" → `They can't all be wrong`
- NO: "A new poll finds 62% support the bill" (a report)

## `authority_appeal`

**Question.** Does the post use an authority as the proof of its claim?

**YES when all are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The author asserts a claim as their own.
3. The post cites a person, an organisation, a study or experts as the support for it: "X confirms", "a study proves", "doctors say so", "according to X, and they are right".

**NO when:**

- the post only attributes a statement and takes no side ("Minister says taxes will rise");
- the post cites someone to criticise them.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Masks don't work. A Stanford study proved it years ago" → `A Stanford study proved it years ago`
- YES: "Top economists confirm it: this tax will kill jobs" → `Top economists confirm it`
- NO: "The minister says the tax will create jobs" (attribution only)

## `unnamed_authority`

**Question.** When the post uses an authority as proof, is that authority left unnamed?

**YES when both are true:**

1. `authority_appeal` is `yes` for this post.
2. The authority cannot be identified from the post: "experts", "scientists", "doctors", "studies", "sources", "insiders", "a friend who works there". No person, organisation or publication is named.

**NO when:**

- `authority_appeal` is `no` or `unclear`;
- the post names a person, an organisation or a publication.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Top economists confirm it: this tax will kill jobs" → `Top economists confirm it`
- NO: "Masks don't work. A Stanford study proved it years ago" (an organisation is named)

## `whataboutism`

**Question.** Does the post answer a criticism by pointing at someone else's wrong instead of addressing it?

**YES when all are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post shows the criticism it reacts to, or refers to it ("you complain about X", "they attack us for X", "all this outrage about X").
3. Instead of addressing it, the post points at a wrong done by the critic or by another party: "what about …", "but when X did …", "where was the outrage when …".

**NO when:**

- the post addresses the criticism and also compares;
- the post only compares two things, with no criticism to answer.

**Examples.**

- YES: "You complain about our minister's expenses? What about your party's private jets?" → `What about your party's private jets?`
- YES: "All this outrage about the leak, but where was it when they spied on journalists?" → `where was it when they spied on journalists?`
- NO: "Both parties overspent, ours less than theirs" (a comparison, no deflection)

## `straw_man`

**Question.** Does the post attack an extreme version of a position that it puts in an opponent's mouth?

**YES when all are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post attributes a position to an opponent: "so you're saying …", "the left thinks …", "they want …", "according to them …".
3. The position is put in an absolute or absurd form: "abolish all …", "let anyone …", "ban everything …".
4. The post attacks or mocks that position.

**NO when:**

- the post quotes the opponent's words exactly;
- the attributed position is specific and measured ("they want to raise the tax by 2 points").

**Examples.**

- YES: "So the greens want us all to live in caves and eat insects. Brilliant" → `the greens want us all to live in caves and eat insects`
- YES: "According to them, anyone who locks their door is a racist" → `anyone who locks their door is a racist`
- NO: "The greens want to ban short flights under 500 km. I disagree" (specific and measured)

## `false_dilemma`

**Question.** Does the post present two options as the only ones?

**YES when both are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post presents exactly two options, or two camps, as the only possible ones: "either … or", "you're with us or against us", "if you are not X, you are Y", "there are only two choices".

**NO when:**

- the choice really has two options (a yes or no referendum);
- the post asks for a preference between two things ("tea or coffee?").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Either you support the strike or you side with the bosses" → `Either you support the strike or you side with the bosses`
- YES: "If you're not angry, you're part of the problem" → `If you're not angry, you're part of the problem`
- NO: "On Sunday, vote yes or no on the new constitution" (a real binary)

## `causal_oversimplification`

**Question.** Does the post give a single cause for a broad outcome?

**YES when all are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post explains a broad outcome: a crisis, crime, prices, a war, a decline, a social problem.
3. It gives one cause, one person or one group, in explicit single-cause wording: "X is the reason for", "all because of", "the only reason", "it's simple: X".

**NO when:**

- the outcome is one specific event with one plain cause ("the flight was cancelled because of the storm");
- the post names several causes, or hedges ("one of the reasons").

**Examples.**

- YES: "Rents are insane for one reason only: immigration" → `for one reason only: immigration`
- YES: "Crime is up because of this mayor. That's it" → `because of this mayor. That's it`
- NO: "Rents rose for several reasons, including supply and rates" (several causes)

## `thought_terminating_cliche`

**Question.** Does the post use a stock phrase to close a debated point?

**YES when both are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post uses a stock phrase to end or dismiss the discussion of a debated point: "it is what it is", "end of story", "period", "simple as that", "that's just how it is", "get over it", "wake up", "do your own research", "facts don't care about your feelings".

**NO when:**

- the phrase is about the author's private life (S is not met);
- the phrase is followed by an argument that carries on the discussion.

**Examples.**

- YES: "Borders are borders. End of story" → `End of story`
- YES: "They lost the election. Get over it" → `Get over it`
- NO: "Prices went up, it is what it is, but here is what the data says about why" (the discussion goes on)

## `nazi_comparison`

**Question.** Does the post discredit a present-day target by likening it to a hated regime or figure?

**YES when both are true:**

1. S is met.
2. The post compares a present-day person, group, policy or act with Nazis, Hitler, fascists, the Gestapo, the Stasi, Stalin or a similar hated regime or figure, or calls it by such a name: "just like Hitler", "literal Nazis", "this is 1933 again", "Gestapo tactics".

**NO when:**

- the post is about the historical regime itself;
- the post reports on a group that calls itself by that name.

**Examples.**

- YES: "These lockdown rules are pure Gestapo tactics" → `pure Gestapo tactics`
- YES: "He is a little Hitler in a cheap suit" → `a little Hitler`
- NO: "A documentary on how the Nazis took power in 1933 airs tonight" (the historical regime)
